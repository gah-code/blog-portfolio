# Standardizing Enterprise Redirect Management Through AEM and SEO Enablement

**Problem**  

As the organization expanded its use of Adobe Experience Manager, teams across content, marketing, SEO, and web operations needed a more consistent way to manage redirects during migrations, URL changes, campaign updates, and ongoing site maintenance.

Redirect management had traditionally required coordination across multiple teams and processes. At enterprise scale, inconsistent redirect handling could create broken URLs, unnecessary redirect chains, lost SEO equity, launch risk, and additional operational overhead. As more teams became responsible for working directly within AEM, we needed a workflow that was technically reliable but also understandable and repeatable for non-development teams.

**Action**  

I led research into the ACS AEM Commons Redirect Manager to determine how it could be incorporated into our existing AEM publishing and WebOps workflows.

I investigated how redirect definitions were structured and managed within AEM, evaluated how the tool interacted with existing site architecture and publishing processes, and worked through implementation considerations surrounding source and destination paths, redirect validation, publishing, and ongoing maintenance.

Rather than treating the Redirect Manager as simply another AEM utility, I focused on how it could become part of a standardized operational process.

I developed a repeatable workflow that connected:

- redirect requirements and URL mapping;
- AEM configuration and authoring;
- technical validation;
- SEO review;
- publishing governance;
- launch readiness; and
- post-launch verification.

Once the workflow was understood and validated, I partnered directly with the global SEO team to demonstrate how the Redirect Manager worked and how their team could participate more directly in redirect planning and implementation.

I translated the tool's technical behavior into an operational process the SEO team could understand—showing how redirect mappings could be prepared, reviewed, implemented, validated, and maintained within AEM.

This created a shared language between SEO specialists, CMS publishers, developers, and WebOps teams. Instead of redirect implementation being isolated as a purely technical task, SEO requirements could move through a documented and repeatable workflow within the CMS.

I also helped establish standards around how redirects should be prepared and reviewed so teams could work consistently as adoption of AEM expanded across the organization.

**Result**  

The implementation helped establish a more scalable and standardized approach to enterprise redirect management while strengthening collaboration between SEO, content operations, development, and AEM publishing teams.

By researching the ACS AEM Commons Redirect Manager, validating its technical implementation, documenting the workflow, and demonstrating it to the global SEO organization, I helped turn a specialized AEM capability into a reusable operational practice.

The result was greater visibility into redirect ownership, more consistent implementation, reduced dependence on ad hoc technical handoffs, and stronger protection of URL integrity and SEO value during migrations and production changes.

More importantly, the work supported the organization's broader adoption of AEM by helping teams move beyond simply learning how to author content in the CMS toward understanding how specialized AEM capabilities could be used collaboratively to manage the website as an enterprise platform.
